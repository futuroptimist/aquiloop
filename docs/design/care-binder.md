# Care Binder Design Brief

**Status:** Reference and layout brief; concept approval, specimen collection,
identity verification, care writing, and PDF implementation are pending.
**Initial deliverable:** Five one-page profiles plus one handwritten log in one
six-page, directly printable PDF.

## 1. Purpose, sequence, and guardrails

This binder will make care information easy to scan at the plant, easy to
annotate, and reproducible as a printed document. Work proceeds in this order:

1. approve one visual direction through provisional concept images;
2. collect photographs of the user's actual specimens and their measured
   growing conditions;
3. verify identities and write evidence-based, condition-aware care copy;
4. implement, validate, render, and physically test the six-page PDF.

This brief does not approve a layout or supply final care instructions.
Generated concept plants are placeholders only. Every photograph committed for
the final profiles must show the user's actual plant and have recorded reuse
permission. Pacifica, California is prior context, not a measured site climate
or a substitute for the exposure and indoor/aquarium observations in section 6.

The ordered initial contents are:

1. reported nursery name **Sedum “Love's Fire”** — Lowe's, outdoor grow bag;
2. reported nursery name **Kalanchoe humilis “Desert Surprise”** — Lowe's,
   outdoor grow bag;
3. reported common name **pothos** — indoors;
4. reported common name **bird of paradise** — indoors initially, with a
   possible indoor/outdoor comparison only in a later revision;
5. reported common name **aquarium hornwort**; and
6. one handwritten watering and aquarium-maintenance log.

## 3. Comparable concept directions

Concepts must use the same fictional specimen and content density so the user
can compare structure rather than copy length. Each prompt below therefore asks
for a single *Kalanchoe humilis* “Desert Surprise” page with the same eight
information categories, a source/revision footer, and no usable care claims.
Labels such as “sample text” deliberately prevent concept art from becoming an
unreviewed care source.

### A. Botanical field guide

A calm, archival page: one dominant macro photograph, generous negative space,
serif display naming, sans-serif utility text, fine rules, and a disciplined
two-column facts grid. Optional detail art is limited to a small leaf-margin or
flower diagram when it clarifies identification.

> Create a flat, straight-on mockup of one US Letter portrait plant-care page,
> not a book scene. Use a prominent original-looking macro placeholder of a
> fictional Kalanchoe humilis “Desert Surprise” specimen across the upper third.
> Show the common/cultivar name and italic binomial distinctly. Below, create an
> orderly two-column grid with exactly eight labeled items: Identity; Family,
> origin & habit; Light & acclimation; Soil/substrate; Water trigger & method;
> Temperature & season; Feeding, maintenance & troubleshooting; Trivia. Use
> short “sample text” lines rather than factual advice. Add a tiny leaf-detail
> diagram, compact sources/revision footer, page number, generous left punch
> margin, warm off-white paper, charcoal ink, one muted botanical-green accent,
> 11-point-feeling body text, high contrast, and a grayscale-safe hierarchy.
> Style it as a precise contemporary botanical field guide with delicate rules;
> no handwriting, no decorative border, no extra categories, no care claims.

### B. Modern modular care cards

A brisk utility sheet: photograph plus title band followed by clearly bounded,
equal-status modules. Shape, label, spacing, and typography—not color alone—
identify categories. One small process diagram is allowed only for a procedure
that benefits from sequencing.

> Create a flat, straight-on mockup of one US Letter portrait plant-care page,
> not a device UI. Use a prominent original-looking macro placeholder of a
> fictional Kalanchoe humilis “Desert Surprise” specimen across the upper third.
> Show the common/cultivar name and italic binomial distinctly. Below, create a
> crisp modular grid with exactly eight labeled cards: Identity; Family, origin
> & habit; Light & acclimation; Soil/substrate; Water trigger & method;
> Temperature & season; Feeding, maintenance & troubleshooting; Trivia. Fill
> cards with equally short “sample text” lines, never factual advice. Add a
> compact sources/revision footer and page number. Preserve a generous left
> punch margin, 11-point-feeling body text, high contrast, grayscale-safe card
> outlines and labels, white background, charcoal type, muted teal accent, and
> restrained line icons. No dashboard chrome, gauges, extra categories, or
> care claims.

### C. Annotated specimen notebook

A measured observational page: a large specimen image sits on a quiet grid,
with numbered callouts leading to aligned notes. Handwritten-style accents may
signal observations, but core facts remain typeset and accessible. Optional
detail diagrams should explain a visible structure, not decorate empty space.

> Create a flat, straight-on mockup of one US Letter portrait plant-care page,
> not an open notebook scene. Use a prominent original-looking macro placeholder
> of a fictional Kalanchoe humilis “Desert Surprise” specimen across the upper
> third, with two subtle numbered anatomical callouts. Show the common/cultivar
> name and italic binomial distinctly. Below, create a clean notebook grid with
> exactly eight labeled entries: Identity; Family, origin & habit; Light &
> acclimation; Soil/substrate; Water trigger & method; Temperature & season;
> Feeding, maintenance & troubleshooting; Trivia. Use equally short “sample
> text” lines, not factual advice. Add a tiny labeled leaf-detail sketch and a
> compact typeset sources/revision footer with page number. Preserve a generous
> left punch margin, 11-point-feeling core text, high contrast, useful grayscale,
> cream paper, graphite rules, and one muted rust accent. Handwriting may appear
> only in brief observational annotations; no clutter, extra categories, or
> care claims.

## 4. Page and content contract

### 4.1 Physical and typographic geometry

- Output is PDF on US Letter portrait pages: **8.5 × 11 in** (612 × 792 PDF
  points), with no crop or bleed requirement.
- Reserve **1.0 in at left** from the trimmed edge to live content for three-hole
  punching. Use **0.625 in** top, right, and bottom printer-safe margins. A faint
  background may enter the margins, but no essential ink may do so.
- Keep body copy at **10.5–11 pt** with at least **13.5 pt** leading; captions and
  sources may be **8.5 pt** minimum with at least **10.5 pt** leading. Use
  **24–30 pt** for the common-name title, **14–18 pt** for the binomial, and
  **12–14 pt** for section headings.
- Target at least **4.5:1** text/background contrast. Meaning must survive
  monochrome printing: pair color with headings, rules, patterns, or labels and
  inspect an actual grayscale render.
- Give the macro photograph roughly the upper **3.0–3.5 in** or comparable area,
  without cropping away identifying features. Captions distinguish specimen
  observations from generic diagrams.

### 4.2 Profile information model

Every terrestrial profile reserves concise, separately labeled space for:

1. reported identity, verified binomial/cultivar status, family, origin, and
   growth habit;
2. light/exposure, including a safe acclimation note;
3. soil/substrate and container/drainage context; include composition by volume
   only when supported and label any proposed mix as a starting recipe;
4. observable watering trigger, application method, and conditional—not
   calendar-prescriptive—interval guidance;
5. temperature limits and seasonal adjustments;
6. feeding and routine maintenance;
7. symptom-led troubleshooting, with uncertainty and escalation where needed;
8. short species/genus trivia; and
9. compact numbered sources, revision date/version, reviewer state, and photo
   credit/license.

Hornwort replaces terrestrial assumptions with: verified identity and growth
form; native range/habitat; light; water temperature and chemistry ranges;
floating versus anchored placement; nutrient/carbon context; growth and
trimming; shedding/algae/troubleshooting; aquarium compatibility and cautions;
trivia; and sources/revision/photo credit. It must not contain a soil recipe or
a watering schedule. Optional diagrams are justified only for identification,
placement, pruning, or another relationship that prose cannot show as clearly.

## 5. Identity and evidence ledger

### 5.1 Source hierarchy and claim labels

Names printed on a nursery pot are **reported label names**, not automatically
verified taxonomy. Store reported wording and photographs verbatim in separate
fields from accepted botanical name, cultivar denomination, trademark, patent
identifier/status, and the date/status source was checked. Never convert curly
quotes or retail capitalization into evidence of cultivar registration.

Use botanical authorities for accepted names and native ranges; university
extension or botanic gardens for broad horticultural principles; and an
identifiable breeder/grower plus official patent records for cultivar and patent
claims. Each final claim should be tagged internally as broadly supported,
manufacturer/cultivar-specific, suggested starting point, local adaptation, or
specimen observation. Conflicts stay visible in notes and uncertain claims do
not become imperative instructions.

## 6. Specimen and environment intake

Before identity/care review, collect:

- for every plant: full specimen, close leaf, stem/node or crown, container,
  drainage, and all sides of the nursery/aquarium-store label; capture a color
  reference and ruler where scale or color is diagnostic;
- for grow bags: internal dimensions/nominal volume, material, drainage,
  ingredient labels and approximate volumes for soil and additives, placement,
  measured direct-sun hours, wind/rain exposure, and recent watering/rain log;
- indoors: window direction, measured distance from glass/lights, obstructions,
  supplemental-light schedule, temperature/humidity range, pot and drainage,
  and recent watering response;
- hornwort: tank volume, lighting model/intensity/photoperiod, temperature, pH,
  hardness when known, nitrogen readings, stocking, fertilization/CO2, flow,
  placement, growth/shedding, and maintenance routine; and
- rights metadata: photographer, capture date, permission/license, preferred
  credit, and confirmation that the image may be committed and redistributed.

Unknown values remain “not measured”; Pacifica context must not fill them in.

## 7. Watering and maintenance log contract

Page six is a landscape-like table composed **within the same US Letter portrait
page**. Its five plant columns follow the manifest order. Each event row begins
with a dedicated date/time field and provides, within each plant cell, room for
amount or method plus a brief observation. A plant not watered on that event is
left blank—never filled with a checkmark that could mean either “done” or “not
applicable.”

Each header has a reserved line for “observed interval: ___ days,” visually
subordinate to the instruction **check moisture and plant condition first;
adjust for season and exposure**. Values remain blank during layout work. The
hornwort header instead says **watering: N/A** and offers aquarium-maintenance
notations such as trim, reposition, water change, or observation.

Outdoor Sedum and Kalanchoe cells include a small “rain/amount” writing cue.
Use at least **0.42 in (10.5 mm)** row height, a minimum 9 pt printed header,
strong alternating rules that remain visible in grayscale, and approximately
12–14 usable rows after title, instructions, header, and footer. A print test
with an ordinary pen must confirm that date/time, method/amount, and observation
can be entered without crowding.

## 8. Future implementation and verification contract

### 8.1 Recommended toolchain

Use **Typst** source compiled with a pinned Typst CLI version. Typst is text
based, supports reusable components and data-driven page generation, bundles
cleanly into a reproducible CLI build, and exposes page dimensions/content in a
form suitable for automated PDF inspection. Installation, fonts, templates,
assets, and build scripts are explicitly deferred.

Keep all photographs and fonts local at build time, with license/credit records.
Document the exact Typst version and every validation utility version; provide
one clean-checkout build command with no network dependency after tools are
installed. Generated PDFs and concept imagery are artifacts, not committed
sources.

### 8.2 Manifest and page budgets

Maintain one machine-readable ordered manifest as the sole assembly order. Its
initial entries are the five profiles and log listed in section 1. Each entry
declares `kind`, stable ID, source path, `page_budget`, and required assets.

- Initial profile `page_budget`: exactly 1 page each.
- Initial log `page_budget`: exactly 1 page.
- Initial combined output: exactly 6 pages in manifest order.
- A future multi-page guide must opt in by changing that entry's explicit
  `page_budget` (for example, from 1 to 2) and updating reviewed expectations;
  it must never gain pages merely because content overflowed.

The generator must compile each entry independently and fail when its actual
pages differ from its budget before assembling the combined PDF. Thus a
fortuitous total of six cannot hide one two-page profile plus one missing page.

### 8.3 Automation and acceptance checks

A future GitHub Actions workflow will run on relevant pull-request paths,
relevant pushes to `main`, and `workflow_dispatch`. It will install pinned
dependencies, build from a clean checkout, validate, and upload **one combined
PDF artifact** with a stable descriptive name. It needs read-only repository
contents and artifact upload only—no credential, release, Pages, or publishing
service.

Automated tests must fail for:

- missing manifest content, required text fields, local fonts, images, credits,
  or unresolved asset paths;
- any individual entry outside its declared page budget;
- combined page order/count differing from the manifest (initially exactly six);
- any page not exactly US Letter portrait within a documented point tolerance;
- blank/nearly blank pages, text or drawing bounds outside the live area, and
  detectable overflow/clipping; and
- a fixture containing an intentionally overlong profile that does **not** fail
  the entry-level budget check. This negative test is mandatory; total count
  alone is not an acceptable pagination test.

CI should also render every page to a pinned-resolution raster for review.
Before layout approval, inspect those renders at full page and 100% crop for
clipping, hierarchy, body/source readability, grayscale separation, photo
resolution/crop, left punch clearance, and log handwriting space. Record the
reviewer and revision. After automated and screen review pass, print all six
pages at 100% on an ordinary target printer, punch/file them, write in several
log rows, and record duplex/show-through, trim, contrast, legibility, and punch
clearance. Only that later physical inspection may call the binder
“print-worthy.”

## 9. Exit criteria for this brief

This reference/design phase is complete when the user has reviewed the three
like-for-like concept images and approved a direction or documented a hybrid.
The brief alone does not satisfy that gate. Concept approval, actual specimen
photographs and measurements, final identity/care verification, PDF sources,
automation, rendered inspection, and physical print approval all remain
pending.
