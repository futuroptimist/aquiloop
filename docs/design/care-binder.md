# Care binder design brief

Status: reference and layout brief; concept approval and PDF implementation are
pending. Last reference review: 2026-09-14.

## Purpose and sequence

The first release is one directly printable, six-page binder: five one-page
profiles followed by one handwritten log. The work must remain concept-first:
compare and approve a layout, collect photographs and site conditions, verify
identities and care claims, and only then implement the PDF source. Generated
concept imagery is provisional. Photographs committed for the final profiles
must show the user's actual specimens.

Initial order (and the future manifest's canonical order):

1. Reported nursery name **Sedum “Love's Fire”** — Lowe's, outdoors in a grow
   bag.
2. Reported nursery name **Kalanchoe humilis “Desert Surprise”** — Lowe's,
   outdoors in a grow bag.
3. **Pothos** — indoors.
4. **Bird of paradise** — indoors initially; an indoor/outdoor comparison may
   be designed later.
5. **Aquarium hornwort**.
6. **Watering and aquarium-maintenance log**.

“Pacifica, California” is prior location context, not a measured growing-site
condition. The grow bags are reported to contain soil and unspecified
additives. Bag sizes, recipes, exposures, watering history, indoor conditions,
aquarium parameters, and specimen identities remain inputs to collect—not
facts to infer.

## Comparable concept directions

All concepts show the same fictional planning specimen: one mature pothos leaf
and node, common name “Pothos,” provisional name *Epipremnum aureum*, and eight
short category items (identity, origin/habit, light, substrate, water,
temperature, maintenance, troubleshooting), plus one trivia line, three tiny
source lines, and revision metadata. No prompt supplies care claims for a real
plant.

### A. Botanical field guide

Quiet, archival, and image-led. A full-width macro photograph occupies roughly
the upper 40%; the name block behaves like a plate caption. Fine rules arrange
the factual categories below in a disciplined two-column grid. A single small
node/leaf detail diagram is permitted if it explains identification.

**Image-generation prompt:**

> Create a flat, straight-on mockup of one US Letter portrait plant-care page,
> not a bound book and not a lifestyle scene. Use a botanical field-guide
> direction: warm off-white paper, restrained dark green and charcoal ink,
> subtle fine rules, ample white space, and excellent grayscale contrast. Keep
> a visibly generous blank left punch margin. Across the upper 40%, place one
> prominent documentary macro photograph of a fictional mature pothos leaf and
> node. Show the heading “Pothos” and italic provisional name “Epipremnum
> aureum.” Below, use a clean two-column grid with exactly eight short labeled
> items: Identity, Origin & Habit, Light, Substrate, Water, Temperature,
> Maintenance, Troubleshooting. Add one brief Trivia line, three compact source
> lines, and revision “DRAFT 2026-09-14.” Optionally add one tiny line-art node
> diagram. Use placeholder microcopy, not real care advice. No decorative plant
> species, handwriting, logos, binder rings, shadows, or clipped content.

### B. Modern modular care cards

Direct, contemporary, and highly scannable. The macro remains dominant while
eight equal-status modules use typographic labels rather than color alone.
Rules, spacing, and limited tints establish grouping; iconography is optional
and always paired with words.

**Image-generation prompt:**

> Create a flat, straight-on mockup of one US Letter portrait plant-care page,
> not a device screen. Use a modern modular care-card direction: white ground,
> charcoal text, one muted green accent, rectangular cards with restrained
> corners, consistent spacing, high contrast, and meaningful grayscale output.
> Keep a visibly generous blank left punch margin. Across the upper 40%, place
> one prominent documentary macro photograph of a fictional mature pothos leaf
> and node. Show the heading “Pothos” and italic provisional name “Epipremnum
> aureum.” Below, create a clear two-column grid of exactly eight labeled cards:
> Identity, Origin & Habit, Light, Substrate, Water, Temperature, Maintenance,
> Troubleshooting. Add one brief Trivia line, three compact source lines, and
> revision “DRAFT 2026-09-14.” Use placeholder microcopy, not real care advice.
> Do not rely on icons or color for meaning. No extra species, handwriting,
> logos, binder rings, shadows, dashboard chrome, or clipped content.

### C. Annotated specimen notebook

Personal and observational but controlled. The macro resembles an affixed
specimen photograph; short ruled annotations and callout leaders add warmth.
The eight categories still occupy a regular printed grid, preventing the style
from becoming an illegible scrapbook.

**Image-generation prompt:**

> Create a flat, straight-on mockup of one US Letter portrait plant-care page.
> Use an annotated specimen-notebook direction: pale natural paper, charcoal
> print, muted blue-green ruling, sparse pencil-like callout leaders, and strong
> grayscale legibility. Keep a visibly generous blank left punch margin. Across
> the upper 40%, place one prominent documentary macro photograph of a
> fictional mature pothos leaf and node as if neatly affixed. Show the heading
> “Pothos” and italic provisional name “Epipremnum aureum.” Below, preserve a
> precise two-column printed grid with exactly eight short labeled items:
> Identity, Origin & Habit, Light, Substrate, Water, Temperature, Maintenance,
> Troubleshooting. Add one brief Trivia line, three compact source lines, and
> revision “DRAFT 2026-09-14.” Use placeholder microcopy, not real care advice.

Approval should select one direction or an explicit hybrid and record the
decision before photographs or renderer work begins.

## Page and content contract

### Physical and typographic specification

- Finished page: US Letter portrait, exactly 8.5 × 11 in (612 × 792 PDF points).
- Safe area: 0.85 in left punch margin; 0.55 in top, right, and bottom margins.
  Keep all ink, photos, rules, folios, and crop-sensitive content inside it.
  The left allowance supports common three-hole punching without making the
  outer edge cramped. It is a design clearance, not a request for printed hole
  guides.
- Use a baseline body size of 10.5 pt with at least 13.5 pt leading. Never use
  body copy below 9.5 pt. Suggested sizes are 24–30 pt common name, 13–16 pt
  binomial, 11–12 pt category headings, 8.5–9 pt compact references, and at
  least 8 pt folio/revision text.
- Use high-contrast text, do not encode meaning by color alone, and check a
  grayscale rendering. Photographs require readable tonal separation; rules
  should remain visible on an ordinary monochrome printer.
- The macro photograph is prominent (target 35–42% of usable page area), has a
  short descriptive alt-text equivalent in source metadata, and receives an
  item-level creator/license credit. Crop without hiding diagnostic features.
- A category is one visually bounded item. Prefer short phrases, conditionals,
  and ranges to dense prose. Optional diagrams are justified only for details
  such as a Sedum/Echeveria distinction, stem node, safe pruning point, grow-bag
  moisture-check depth, or hornwort growth/anchoring mode.

### Profile schema

Every terrestrial profile reserves concise, separately labeled items for:

1. **Identity:** reported name, verified botanical name and author where
   useful, cultivar/patent wording and confidence, family, native origin, and
   growth habit.
2. **Light & exposure:** supported light range, observed placement, stress
   signs, rotation if relevant, and staged acclimation to stronger sun.
3. **Soil/substrate:** drainage goals and a practical composition by volume
   only when evidence and the actual materials support it. Label any trial
   recipe as a starting point, not a universal formula.
4. **Water:** observable trigger and check depth, method, drainage, signs of
   excess/deficit, and a conditional—not calendar-guaranteed—interval range.
5. **Temperature & season:** supported range, weather thresholds where sourced,
   frost/heat response, and seasonal changes to water, light, or placement.
6. **Feeding:** product-neutral timing/strength guidance where supported.
7. **Maintenance & troubleshooting:** pruning, propagation or repotting,
   inspection cues, and likely problems without presenting diagnosis as fact.
8. **Trivia:** one sourced species/genus fact, clearly less important than care.
9. **Evidence footer:** compact numbered sources, photo credit/license,
   verification state, revision date, and editor.

For hornwort, replace terrestrial concepts rather than relabeling them:
identity and growth form; lighting; water temperature and supported chemistry
ranges; water-column nutrients/fertilization; flow and placement (floating or
anchored); growth, trimming, and shedding; livestock/ecosystem cautions;
troubleshooting; trivia; and evidence footer. “Watering” is not applicable.
Aquarium maintenance observations belong to the log but must not be presented
as watering events.

## Identity and evidence gate

Nursery text is evidence about a label, not automatically a taxonomic
determination. Store these fields independently: `reported_name`,
`verified_taxon`, `cultivar`, `trade_name`, `patent_statement`, `source`,
`verification_date`, and `confidence`. Preserve label photographs verbatim;
normalize nothing silently. Italicize genus/species, but not cultivar names,
trade names, or patent notices.

### Current findings and open decisions

- **Sedum:** Plants of the World Online (POWO) is the authority to query for
  [*Sedum adolphi* Raym.-Hamet](https://powo.science.kew.org/results?q=Sedum%20adolphi).
  The [GBIF Backbone Taxonomy record](https://www.gbif.org/species/5688173)
  independently returns *Sedum adolphi* Raym.-Hamet as accepted; its name-match
  service maps `Sedum adolphii` only as a fuzzy match to that spelling. Treat
  `Sedum adolphii` as the user's reported spelling pending comparison to the
  physical label, not an accepted name. Also record any synonym shown by the
  primary authority rather than substituting one from memory.
  “Love's Fire” could not be tied to a stable botanical cultivar or patent in
  the reviewed authoritative sources. The inaccessible retailer-aggregation
  page is insufficient to establish that it is *S. adolphi*, a cultivar, or a
  patented plant. “PPAF” means a US plant patent was applied for, not that its
  status remains current; check the label's applicant/breeder and the
  [USPTO Patent Public Search](https://ppubs.uspto.gov/pubwebapp/static/pages/landing.html)
  immediately before publication. Until matched to a patent/application,
  reproduce PPAF only as dated label text. Search also for the distinct retail
  use **Echeveria “Love's Fire”** and compare diagnostic photographs/label
  provenance; shared marketing words do not establish synonymy.
- **Kalanchoe:** retain reported `Kalanchoe humilis “Desert Surprise”` until the
  label and specimen are checked. The [RHS “Desert Surprise” record](https://www.rhs.org.uk/plants/310184/kalanchoe-humilis-desert-surprise/details)
  is an identifiable horticultural source, but it does not replace a label
  photograph or taxonomic verification. Confirm whether RHS styles the quoted
  element as a cultivar and reproduce that form consistently.
- **Pothos:** [NC State Extension's *Epipremnum aureum* profile](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/epipremnum-aureum/)
  supports *E. aureum* as a plausible identity and supplies extension-level
  descriptive/care evidence. It remains provisional until photographs of
  leaves, petioles, nodes, habit, and any label exclude other plants sold as
  pothos.
- **Bird of paradise:** do not choose a species from the common name. Compare
  the label and diagnostic specimen features against the separate NC State
  Extension profiles for [*Strelitzia reginae*](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/strelitzia-reginae/)
  and [*Strelitzia nicolai*](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/strelitzia-nicolai/),
  then confirm the accepted name in POWO. The indoor/outdoor comparison is a
  later option, not a claim that the specimen tolerates either placement.
- **Hornwort:** treat *Ceratophyllum demersum* as provisional. Compare the
  specimen and seller label with POWO and the identifiable aquarium grower's
  [Tropica hornwort profile](https://tropica.com/en/plants/plantdetails/?id=4434).
  Record whether it arrived rooted, weighted, floating, or loose and do not
  infer aquarium parameters from the product page.

The two supplied Greg/Oasis-label pages—[Sedum “Love's Fire”](https://greg.app/brand/oasis-labels/sedum-loves-fire-010444/)
and [Kalanchoe “Desert Surprise”](https://greg.app/brand/oasis-labels/kalanchoe-humilis-desert-surprise-010315/)—returned
HTTP 403 during both the initial review and the 2026-09-14 follow-up. Their
content and provenance remain unverified. Keep the links as leads, never as the
sole support for an identity or care claim.

### Evidence rules

Use POWO or another botanical authority for accepted names and synonyms;
university extension and botanic gardens for broadly supported horticultural
principles; and an identifiable breeder/grower plus patent registry for a
named selection. Record access date and the claim each citation supports.
Resolve conflicts explicitly in notes; do not average incompatible ranges.

Label advice as one of: **supported principle**, **suggested starting point**,
or **local/specimen observation**. Calendar intervals and soil ratios can be
starting points only after container, materials, exposure, season, and measured
behavior are known. Never invent a universal recipe, schedule, local climate
measurement, or specimen fact.

Before final writing, collect:

- front/back label and receipt/nursery details, including patent/trademark text;
- sharp whole-plant and macro photographs with scale and photo credits;
- grow-bag dimensions/material, ingredient labels and approximate volumes,
  drainage, measured exposure, rain/irrigation history, and moisture checks;
- indoor window direction/distance, supplemental lights, temperature/humidity
  observations, pot dimensions, drainage, medium, and watering history; and
- aquarium volume, light and photoperiod, temperature, pH/hardness if measured,
  stocking, filtration/flow, fertilizers, placement, and maintenance history.

## Handwritten log contract

Page six is landscape-like information arranged on the required portrait
sheet. Its left stub has **Date/time** and **event-wide notes**. Five plant
columns follow in manifest order; each dated row is one event and unwatered
plants remain blank. Each terrestrial cell supports a compact amount or method
plus observation. The hornwort cell is labeled **Aquarium maintenance / obs.**,
not watering, and may record trim, water change, dosing, shedding, or growth.

Each column header provides a blank `Typical interval: ____ days` field,
visually subordinate to this printed instruction: **Check moisture and plant
condition first; adjust for season, exposure, rain, container, and growth.**
Hornwort instead prints `Watering interval: N/A`. Do not prefill intervals in
the layout phase.

Reserve at least 0.42 in (30 pt) row height, target 12–14 usable rows, and avoid
gray fills that resist pencil. Outdoor Sedum and Kalanchoe cells include a
small `Rain:` writing cue. Provide a 0.8 in minimum notes area at the bottom for
seasonal changes and aquarium parameters. Validate all fields with an actual
pen on a physical print.

## Future implementation and verification contract

### Recommended toolchain

Use **LuaLaTeX** with a small data-driven source layer (for example, individual
`.tex` profile records included by one ordered manifest). It offers exact page
geometry, strong print typography, local OpenType fonts, stable image handling,
and mature PDF output while keeping the authoritative source editable and
reviewable. Installation, templates, content, assets, and automation are
explicitly deferred. Pin the TeX Live release and package versions (or a
container digest) when implementation begins.

### Build contract

- Maintain one ordered manifest containing profiles and supplemental pages;
  generated file discovery or filename sorting must not determine order.
- Produce one combined PDF, initially exactly six pages in the order above.
- Compile and validate each initial species profile independently with an exact
  one-page budget. Give every manifest entry an explicit `page_budget` so a
  later multi-page guide may intentionally declare 2 or more without weakening
  the initial constraints. The log initially has a one-page budget too.
- Keep fonts, final cleared photographs, and other assets local. Fail on missing
  content, missing assets, missing glyphs, unresolved references, or font
  substitution. Document deterministic build commands, tool/package versions,
  license/credit records, and supported clean-build environment.
- Do not commit the generated PDF unless a later decision changes artifact
  policy. Do not require credentials or a publishing service.

### Automation and regression contract

A later GitHub Actions workflow must run for relevant pull-request paths,
relevant changes on `main`, and `workflow_dispatch`. It builds once from a
clean checkout and uploads one clearly named, downloadable combined-PDF
artifact.

Automated checks must:

1. compile every manifest entry alone and assert its declared page budget;
2. assert combined order and exact initial count of six (using machine-readable
   page markers as well as count);
3. inspect every media box as US Letter portrait within a documented tolerance;
4. fail for absent required fields, sources, photos, credits, fonts, or assets;
5. fail for blank pages, unintended overflow pages, overfull boxes, clipped
   safe-area content, unresolved references, and substituted/missing glyphs;
6. include a fixture with an intentionally overlong profile and assert that the
   entry-level pagination check fails—passing a six-page total alone is not an
   adequate test; and
7. keep artifact names and manifest ordering deterministic.

After automation, rasterize every page at print-representative resolution and
inspect at 100% for clipping, small type, hierarchy, photograph crop/resolution,
grayscale contrast, punch clearance, blank/overflow pages, and log handwriting
space. Approval of screen renders is not “print-worthy”: conduct and record a
later physical US Letter print inspection, including three-hole punching and a
handwritten log row.
