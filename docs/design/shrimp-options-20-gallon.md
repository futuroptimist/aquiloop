# Freshwater shrimp options for the 20-gallon tank

Status: K220 comparison for discussion, researched 2026-10-04. No species,
stocking count, purchase, or tank modification has been selected.

Start with the animals Daniel would enjoy and the conditions they need; then
verify or plan a suitable home. Unknown current chemistry does not prevent this
comparison. Daniel reports a filtered 20-gallon freshwater tank with an air
stone, guppy grass, hornwort, and two Kuhli loaches established for about three
years. Tank dimensions, actual temperature, pH, GH, KH, and other occupants have
not been verified. The existing loaches remain part of every suitability decision.

## Options and their tradeoffs

**The main discussion is colorful, reproducing Neocaridina versus larger Amano
grazers.** Both are reasonable hardy starting options when their requirements
are met. Bamboo shrimp offer different feeding behavior; identified freshwater
ghost shrimp offer transparency and larval development; crystal/bee shrimp offer
patterned dwarf shrimp with more specialized water management. This is a shortlist,
not a claim that every freshwater species has been assessed.

Mixing *N. davidi* colors can produce less predictable, eventually brown/gray
offspring; choose one line if preserving a color matters. Hornwort and other
plants provide refuge and grazing surfaces. Neither an expensive grade nor a
cheap feeder label is a health guarantee. [Neocaridina specialist][neo-farm]

## Required water conditions: ranges are not setpoints

These are **published husbandry ranges**, largely from specialist retailers with
keeping experience, not controlled lethal-limit experiments. They are not proof
that every value inside a row is equally desirable or that abrupt movement across
it is safe. GH and KH below are degrees (dGH/dKH), not ppm or TDS.

For **Neocaridina plus the existing loaches**, a possible temperature discussion
band is 74–76 °F: the intersection of the cherry guide's preferred 72–76 °F and
the Kuhli guide's 74–80 °F. This is an explicit planning derivation, not a measured
tank condition or an instruction to change the heater. Amano's listed 70–80 °F
also includes this band. There is no similarly established shared pH/GH/KH target
for Daniel's unidentified *Pangio* stock in these sources; do not manufacture one
by intersecting unrelated retailers' tolerance endpoints. [Kuhli care][kuhli]

GH concerns principally calcium and magnesium; KH concerns carbonate/bicarbonate
buffering. Measure each separately. A pH result alone does not show whether the
water has appropriate mineral content, and TDS is not a substitute for either
test. [GH explanation][gh] [KH explanation][kh]

## Shared care and compatibility

**Cycling and stability.** The three-year history is encouraging but does not
certify today's water. Before adding animals, require ammonia and nitrite to be
undetectable on functioning aquarium tests. Record nitrate and its reporting unit,
source-water baseline, and trend; maintain it low with an appropriate maintenance
plan. This comparison does not establish a universal shrimp nitrate toxicity
threshold. The ghost guide explicitly calls for zero ammonia/nitrite and low
nitrate. [Ghost care][ghost] Preserve biological filtration and avoid abrupt
chemistry corrections: veterinary guidance notes that raising pH rapidly in an
old aquarium can increase ammonia toxicity. [Veterinary management][vet]

**Copper, medication, and molting.** Review prior treatments, proposed medications,
and water products before introducing invertebrates. Copper treatments can be
highly toxic to invertebrates and can damage the biofilter; a fish-safe treatment
is not automatically shrimp-safe. Seek an aquatic veterinarian's guidance when
the established fish need treatment. [Veterinary management][vet] Provide cover
for soft, freshly molted shrimp and a varied shrimp-appropriate diet; do not treat
every failed molt as proof that a mineral additive is needed. Check water and
feeding first. [Cherry care][cherry]

**Food, plants, and equipment.** Retain mature grazing surfaces and offer small,
observed supplemental meals rather than assuming algae or fish leftovers suffice.
Amano shrimp can take food quickly, so verify that bottom feeders still receive
their ration. Fit accessible filter intakes with suitable shrimp protection;
maintain flow as the guard collects debris. Keep plant cover, shelter, and a secure
lid. These are habitat provisions, not guarantees of survival. [Amano care][amano]
[Neocaridina specialist][neo-farm] For bamboo shrimp, check actual fan-feeding
success; repeated substrate scraping can indicate inadequate suspended food.
More powdered food must not become an excuse for deteriorating water quality.
[Bamboo guide][bamboo-guide]

**Kuhli loaches and fish.** The Kuhli care guide describes a peaceful fish and says
shrimp predation is not its usual experience; that is practical keeper evidence,
not a controlled estimate of zero risk. Larger healthy adult shrimp are a more
plausible community prospect than tiny juveniles. Juvenile predation and losses
during molts remain risks inferred from size and vulnerability; no quantified
Kuhli-specific loss rate was established here. Dense cover can help but cannot
promise colony growth. Continue targeted sinking food for the loaches rather
than relying on scavenging. [Kuhli care][kuhli] Even small peaceful fish may eat
baby dwarf shrimp; a shrimp-focused tank is the more controllable breeding choice.
Avoid fish large enough to eat adult shrimp, including predatory puffers and many
larger cichlids. [Neocaridina specialist][neo-farm] [Amano specialist][amano-farm]

The same Kuhli guide recommends a group of 3–6 or more in at least 20 gallons.
Daniel's two are below that social-group guidance. This warrants a separate review
of species, tank footprint, cover, health, other stock, and capacity; it does not
authorize adding more loaches or shrimp now. [Kuhli care][kuhli]

**Reproduction is a choice.** Neocaridina can make a freshwater colony, but plan
for surplus animals and uncertain juvenile survival with fish. Amano larvae have
a saline phase: a peer-reviewed experiment found larval adaptation at 17–34 ppt
under its tested conditions. That supports the life-cycle distinction, not salting
this freshwater tank or a complete home breeding recipe. [Larval study][larvae]
Bamboo also needs a separate larval project. Never salt the loaches' aquarium to
raise either species' larvae. [Bamboo profile][bamboo]

## Choose an option, then verify a suitable setup

1. Choose the desired experience: one colorful breeding line, larger algae grazers
   without freshwater recruitment, or the extra work of fan feeding. Neocaridina
   and Amano are the initial discussion pair; neither is selected by this document.
2. Confirm species/line with a supplier, its actual holding water, animal size and
   age if known, and health/observation arrangements. For ghost shrimp, reject an
   ambiguous common-name identification. Plan acclimation with the supplier based
   on transport and water differences rather than prescribing one universal drip
   duration. [Amano specialist][amano-farm]
3. Before implementation, record tank dimensions and stock; temperature including
   daily/seasonal variation; pH, GH, KH, ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate in tank and
   replacement water. Include filtration/intake details and medication history.
   These are setup-verification inputs, not prerequisites to discussing options.
4. Compare the chosen species' requirements with the loaches' needs and a sustainable
   replacement-water routine. If they conflict, consider a separate shrimp tank
   or a different option. Do not abruptly alter established fish water to satisfy
   a table, and do not use live shrimp as a cycling test.
5. Confirm feeding, cover, intake protection, observation, and a surplus/rehome plan
   before deciding a stocking count or buying animals. This research does not yet
   establish a safe count for the actual tank.
