\CareRow{LIGHT / EXPOSURE}{Full light indoors. Outdoors, start in full shade and increase light over 10–14 days; use a sunny, sheltered final site only if response is good. \textbf{[KAL-RHS; KAL-OUT]}}{SOIL / SUBSTRATE}{Starting example: peat-free loam-based potting compost plus grit, in a draining pot that never stands in water. RHS gives no ratio. \textbf{[KAL-RHS; KAL-IA]}}
\CareRow{WATER}{Let medium dry between thorough waterings without waiting for wilt; drain fully. Reduce only when winter light and growth are lower. \textbf{[KAL-RHS; KAL-IA]}}{TEMPERATURE / SEASON}{H1b is a 10–15°C \emph{minimum-temperature category}, not an ideal range: indoors/under glass except warm summer in a sunny, sheltered site; protect from cold. \textbf{[KAL-RHS; KAL-HARD]}}
\CareRow{FEEDING / MAINTENANCE}{Candidate: liquid feed 2–3 times in the growing season; no routine pruning. Keep from chewing pets; Kalanchoe spp. are toxic to cats/dogs. \textbf{[KAL-RHS; KAL-ASPCA]}}{PROPAGATION}{In spring/summer, remove lower leaves from a clean stem section; callus, then use well-drained medium. An uncallused cutting may rot. \textbf{[KAL-RHS; KAL-PROP]}}
\CareRow{TROUBLESHOOTING}{Mealybugs/aphids or leaf disease are possible; inspect before diagnosing. Also check wet medium, drainage, and cold exposure. \textbf{[KAL-RHS; KAL-IA]}}{NATURAL HISTORY / TRIVIA}{RHS describes the candidate as a compact succulent with rounded, fleshy olive-green leaves patterned dark red—not a specimen observation. \textbf{[KAL-RHS]}}
